## The change-management conversation

### Confirm the actual setup

What runs the staff tee sheet, checkout, payments, website, member accounts and distribution? Which tools are separate? Ask directly; do not infer the entire stack from a booking page.

### Find a reason to change

What works well today? What specific problem would make a review worthwhile? Ask how often it happens and what it costs in time or disruption. If there is no meaningful reason to change, respect that.

### Protect the operation

List must-keep workflows and people affected: league blocks, member rates, nine-hole routing, future reservations, prepaid bookings, gift cards, rain checks and reports. Confirm which apply at this course.

### Map the decision and timing

Who approves, who uses it daily, and who can stop the change? Ask about renewal, notice periods, data access and exports, hardware, payments and bundled services. Do not assume fees or a right to cancel.

### Earn an evaluation

Propose a short requirements session with the operator and a staff champion. Agree on representative scenarios and success criteria before a demonstration. Then validate product fit, data handling, training, cutover ownership and a fallback plan before committing to any rollout.

### Use the off-season sensibly

Ask for their actual planning window. League setup, prepaid reservations and year-round operations can make a winter change disruptive too. The goal now is a qualified conversation, not an artificial deadline to switch.


## How to work the list

### Before calling

Check existing CRM history, replies, opt-outs, and account ownership. This research does not establish which courses have already been contacted. Start with the first 15, then the four Metro Detroit contact-confirmation calls, then geographic expansion.

### At the front desk

Hi, this is Billy with TeeAhead, a Metro Detroit tee sheet software company. Your website lists [published name and role]. Are they still the right person to speak with about booking software? If not, who is? For unnamed accounts, ask for the current golf operations manager without guessing a name.

### When someone answers

Start by confirming the recorded system and its scope. Use the course-specific opening, then listen. Ask what works today, what would need to improve, who approves a change, and when the existing agreement renews. A question in this playbook is a hypothesis to explore, not a claim that the course has a problem.

### Get a concrete next step

Ask for 15 minutes with the operator who uses the system. Offer to review one sample workflow and return a short requirements summary. If timing is wrong, record a specific month and permission to follow up.

### After the call

Log the correct name and role, source and confirmation date, phone/email supplied, decision authority, current system as stated, the real problem, next action, due date, and Billy as owner. Use the course-specific email only where appropriate; obtain an address first when none is published.

### If they say send information

Absolutely. Which booking task should I focus on so I can send something useful? If they have no specific issue, send a short introduction and ask whether a later conversation is welcome.

### If they are happy or under contract

Respect the answer. Ask whether they want a comparison before their next review or renewal. Do not claim that changing is easy, promise savings, or keep contacting someone who declines.

### Claims to keep accurate

Position TeeAhead as a local tee sheet software company. Do not promise a golfer audience, integrations, migrations, 27-hole routing, marketing activation, or financial results without verification. Discuss founding-partner pricing only after confirming current written terms. No campaign has been sent and no contacts have been imported by this work.
